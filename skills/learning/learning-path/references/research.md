# Researching the field

Read this before the first search, whether that is to find concrete versions of an undefined goal or to research a defined one. `SKILL.md` sets the order and when to stop; this is the craft behind them. Most of it exists because the easy version of each step produces a confident plan built on something nobody checked.

## Dates

- Record two dates for every source: when it was published or last updated, or its version, and the day you read it.
- Find the version of the standard in force today, and the one that will be in force on their deadline. Boards and regulators usually announce changes ahead of time. Search for the standard's name with "changes", "new specification", "from 2027", "consultation" or "withdrawn".
- For software, use the current release and the documentation for that version.
- Age alone does not make a source wrong. Give the age of anything more than a year old, and say why it still holds.

## Who sets the standard

Find this before anything else. Searches that usually surface it: the goal plus "official syllabus", "specification", "exam handbook", "assessment criteria", "requirements", "regulator", "competency framework", "professional body" or "governing body".

Some frameworks span many fields. Check the current version of any you use:

- The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) describes six language levels, A1 to C2, as can-do statements.
- The Skills Framework for the Information Age (SFIA) sets digital skills against seven levels of responsibility.
- ESCO (European Skills, Competences, Qualifications and Occupations) describes occupations and the skills they need, for the EU.
- O*NET (the Occupational Information Network) does the same for the US.
- The European Qualifications Framework (EQF) compares qualifications across eight levels.

A framework names a level. The assessment that tests it is still the standard.

For a job goal, the standard is what employers ask for. Read the postings in full, quote their requirement lines, count how many name each skill (8 of 12), and date each one.

## The source ladder

Work down it. Where two rungs disagree, the higher one wins, and the plan says so.

1. **The body that sets the fact, speaking about it.** That is the standard-setter on its own syllabus, specification, handbook or marking criteria, or a regulator on its rules. It is also a publisher on its official documentation, an employer on its own posting, and a seller on its own price and edition. Confirmed on its own for that fact. Its guidance on hours counts, as its estimate. Outcomes it sells do not, such as pass rates after its own course.
2. **Research and official data.** Peer-reviewed studies and systematic reviews, official statistics, published pass rates, examiner and assessor reports. One such source confirms what that study or dataset found. Write the claim that way, not as a general law.
3. **Named practitioners.** People with a checkable record in the field, writing about their own practice. That means professional blogs, conference talks, professional associations, and textbooks used on accredited courses. Record the author, their standing and the date.
4. **Crowd accounts.** Forums, review sites, interview reports. Unconfirmed until two independent accounts agree.

Not sources:

- search results pages;
- answers from AI tools, yours included;
- pages with no author and no date that exist to rank in search;
- course sellers describing their own results;
- affiliate "best of" lists.

Your memory is not a source either. It can say where to look. It cannot say what the page says today.

## Independence

Two sources are independent when neither merely repeats the other, and they share no owner and no commercial interest. A review that re-analyses many studies is independent of each of them. Two articles summarising the same study are one source: find the study.

## How deep

- **Read the standard itself**, not someone's summary of it.
- **Read how it is judged**: the criteria, the format, the conditions, and at least one real sample or past assessment.
- **Find where people fail.** Examiner reports, assessor feedback, pass rates by component. The path weighs most where candidates lose most.
- **Find the effort evidence.** Official guided hours, a regulator's minimum training hours, published studies. A practitioner's estimate stays unconfirmed unless corroborated. If nothing exists, say so; the path's hours are then your estimate, labelled, with its basis.
- **Find the prerequisites** the standard assumes and does not teach.
- **Check every resource** the path will use. It exists, its edition or version is current and matches the standard's, and you know what it costs. Note whether the standard-setter recommends it.

## The evidence ledger

Every fact the plan uses gets a row as it is found, not afterwards. The table goes into the plan as its Evidence section.

| Column | Holds |
| :--- | :--- |
| ID | E1, E2, and so on. Milestones cite these. |
| Claim | One fact, in your words. |
| Quote | The source's exact words that support it. |
| Source | Title and link, or a DOI written as a link. A second link when a second source confirms it. |
| Tier | 1 to 4 from the ladder, or `learner` for their own evidence. |
| Published | A date, a version, or `undated`. With two sources, the stronger one's. |
| Checked | The day you read it, as YYYY-MM-DD. |
| Status | `confirmed` or `unconfirmed`. |

- **Confirmed** means a tier-1 or tier-2 source on its own fact, or two independent sources. Anything else is unconfirmed. An unconfirmed row can shape a milestone and cannot carry one.
- **A count is one row.** The claim is the count, and the Source lists every posting or item read. The Quote gives the line from each one that names the thing counted.
- **A `learner` row** records their own evidence, with no link. Its Source says "their account" or "diagnostic", with the date. It is confirmed only for a result you watched or marked yourself. It never carries the standard or a milestone.
- **An unreliable read stays unconfirmed.** Sometimes two reads of a page disagree, or the tool returns a summary instead of the text. The row stays unconfirmed until the exact words are found or a second source settles it.

## When research fails

If the standard cannot be found or verified, nothing can be built on it. The verdict is NO PATH, with what was searched. Say what would settle it, such as a question to the exam board or a teacher's written statement.
