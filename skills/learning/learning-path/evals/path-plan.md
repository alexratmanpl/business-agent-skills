As of: 2026-09-25
Verdict: PATH
Effort: 64 h (estimate: 16 weeks at 4 h a week, the pace E2 gives)

## Goal
- Performance: Run 21.1 km on a road course in under 2:00:00 without stopping
- Standard: A chip time under 2:00:00 on a course measured to the official distance (E1)
- Conditions: Race day, road surface, official timing, no pacer carrying the runner
- Deadline: 2027-01-31
- Hours a week: 5
- Starting point: Runs 5 km three times a week in about 30 minutes each (E4)
- Use: A personal target

## Verdict
The evidence puts a first half marathon at about 64 hours (E2). Five hours a week for the 18 weeks to the deadline gives 91 hours, so it fits with more than a week to spare.

## Path
### M1. Diagnostic: a timed 10 km
- Outcome: Run 10 km on a measured course and record the finish time and average heart rate
- Practice: One timed effort in week 1, then repeat in week 8, with the watch file kept
- Check: Compare the finish time with the pace a 2:00:00 half marathon needs, judged by the runner against the plan's table
- Evidence: E1, E4
- Time: 3 h (estimate: two timed efforts and warm-ups)

### M2. Long run of 16 km
- Outcome: Run 16 km continuously at an even pace under 6:10 per km on a measured route
- Practice: One long run a week, adding one kilometre a week, with an easy week every fourth week
- Check: The watch file shows the distance and the pace, checked by the runner against the target pace
- Evidence: E2, E3
- Time: 61 h (E2)

## Checks
- Rubric: Even pace within 10 seconds per km across the second half of the long run (anchors written by the plan author)
- Reviews: Read each attempt against the rubric, name the one change to make, and set the next attempt
- Exit check: A timed 21.1 km time trial three weeks before the race, on a route not used in training
- Re-plan when: Two checks are missed in a row, hours fall a week behind, or the race date moves
- Spaced review: Record each recurring mistake in the same words and revisit it every two weeks

- Whether the target pace is realistic for this runner: only the diagnostic can show it.
