# Company Research

Find out what a company is really like, beyond what it says about itself.

## Asking

Use whatever interactive choice mechanism the environment offers — tappable options where they exist, plain questions otherwise. One round, three questions at most.

**If nobody is watching** — a background job, a scheduled run, a delegated task in an agentic workspace — do not stall waiting for an answer. Take the most useful default, state the assumption plainly in the output, and carry on.

## Ask first

- Which company (disambiguate common names).
- Why they're asking — joining, buying, selling, investing, competing. This decides what matters.
- How deep.

## Find

Sources and how far to trust them: `references/where-to-look.md`.

**What they do.** The real product and the real buyer, not the slogan. If you can't say in two sentences who writes the cheque and why, keep reading. No pricing page usually means long enterprise contracts. No named customers means very early or unable to get permission — both matter.

**Money.** Funding with amounts, dates, investors. Revenue and profit claims, and who makes them. For young companies: how long the money lasts. For established: results, layoffs, lawsuits. Compare money raised against staff numbers.

**Competitors.** Similar-sounding companies often sell to entirely different buyers — for each, ask who signs the cheque and what problem is solved. If those differ, they aren't competitors. Note who has far more money, and who has bought whom.

**Self-descriptions side by side.** Collect each competitor's one-line pitch. Where they cluster is industry consensus; where one differs is a deliberate bet. Asking a company about its bet is the best available question.

**Working there.** Reviews, including local-language sites. Read by department. Always give sample size and dates. No reviews means "no information", not a verdict.

**People.** Leadership backgrounds and tenure. Cross-check the founding story; if it changes, ask about it. Advisers matter at small companies — they open doors to customers.

## Write the dossier

Save as `company-dossier-NAME.md` and tell them the filename, so they can point another skill at it later. Sections: what they do, money, competitors, what makes them different, working there, people, risks, what I couldn't find out.

- **Plain words.** No jargon or abbreviations without explanation. "Money raised from investors", not "Series B".
- **Mark confidence.** Facts get a source and date. Company claims get "they say". Estimates get "roughly". Never let a guess look like a fact.
- **Gaps are findings.** End with what you couldn't establish.

## Rules

- Prefer originals: announcements, filings, regulators, court records, accounts.
- Market analysis published by a company selling into that market is advertising. Check whose site you're on.
- Rankings are often paid or self-submitted. Aggregator revenue estimates are guesses. Label both.
- Conflicting sources: give both.
- Wrong earlier? Correct it plainly.
