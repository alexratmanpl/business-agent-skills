# The three-to-five year read

Read this at the step that writes the horizon half. It is the half that makes the report
worth keeping, and the half most likely to turn into fortune-telling. The discipline that
prevents it:

- **Every shift must already be visible in something dated.** Four places a real one
  shows: a law with a compliance date, money moving into teams or procurement, work
  advertised under no settled title yet, and anything employers now pay a premium for that
  was free last year. Retirement curves are slower and more reliable than all four.
- **Name the mechanism, not the trend.** "This field will grow" asserts nothing. "This
  rule applies from that date, and somebody has to produce the evidence trail for it" can
  be checked, and points at who will be hiring.
- **Write what would prove it wrong.** If nothing could, cut the shift. A forecast nobody
  can lose is worth nothing to someone deciding what to learn.
- **Then apply the ageing test to each asset.** Does it get more valuable as they get more
  senior, or less? Judgement, accountability, domain knowledge and being trusted with a
  system age well. Raw output speed, one vendor's tooling and being the newest at
  something age badly. This is the axis that matters most to anyone past forty.
- **Two clocks, running together.** The fast one pays and builds the record the slow one
  needs. The slow one is whatever cannot be bought later — a language, a qualification, a
  public record, a gap that only closes with repetition. Give both, and say which gates
  which.
- **Put dates on the gates.** What closes, what enrols, what expires.
