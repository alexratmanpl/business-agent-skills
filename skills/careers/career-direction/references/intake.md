# The intake, pass by pass

Read this at the step that asks them for their history. `SKILL.md` says when to stop
and what to do with a contradiction; this says what each pass digs for.

A shallow intake produces a reading anybody could have written from a job title.

Run the passes in this order, and say what each one is for. Skip what is already answered.

**1. The record.** Every role with dates, then the systems, the scale, the money, the
people. Ask for the CV, read it, then ask what it leaves out. The CV is the artefact that
has been failing them; treat it as evidence about their self-description rather than about
their work.

**2. What other people said.** Written references, review quotes, promotion notes, what
colleagues come to them for, and the rejection letters. Ask for the words, not the gist.
This pass outranks everything they say about themselves, and rejection letters are the
only place some employers say what was actually missing. Where a job platform already
scores their profile against postings, ask them to read back the score and what it lists
as missing. It is free and specific, and it measures how they are indexed rather than how
they work — a different gap from the rest of this pass, and usually a cheaper one to close.

**3. What is not on the record.** Side projects, earlier careers, freelance and voluntary
work, the thing they built that nobody asked for, the languages, the failure they learned
from. People leave out the most relevant thing they have done because it does not match
their current title. Ask directly, more than once.

**4. Energy and interest.** What they read when nobody is paying, what they would do
unpaid, which part of the last job they would keep and which they would not repeat. Ask
what drains them; that decides more than what they are good at, and almost nobody
volunteers it.

**5. Constraints, as numbers.** Income floor as a figure rather than a feeling. Runway in
months, notice period, cities and countries in and out, travel they will accept. Work
rights, languages at working level, family and health. On-call, quota-carrying work,
employment against contracting, and the hours a week available for the search and for any
build.

**6. The clock.** Their age, what they intend to be doing at the end of the next decade,
and what they are not willing to still be doing. Direction that ignores this ages badly in
exactly the way they are afraid of.

Treat what they say they want as a hypothesis, and test it against passes 1 to 4.
Sometimes the record says something the person does not.
