# What each intake pass digs for

**1. The record.** Every role with dates, then the systems, the scale, the money handled,
the people. Ask for the CV, read it, then ask what it leaves out. The CV is the artefact
that has been failing them; treat it as evidence about their self-description rather than
about their work.

**2. What other people said.** Written references, review quotes, promotion notes, what
colleagues come to them for, and the rejection letters. Ask for the words, not the gist. A
remark they relay from memory is their account of someone else's words: mark it as relayed,
and ask who could confirm it; unconfirmed, it counts as theirs. Two people at one employer
are one organisation, and count as one source. This pass outranks everything they say about
themselves, and rejection letters are the only place some employers say what was actually
missing. Where a job platform already scores their profile against postings, ask them to
read back the score and what it lists as missing. It is free and specific, and it measures
how they are indexed rather than how they work — a different gap from the rest of this pass,
and usually a cheaper one to close.

**3. What is not on the record.** Side projects, earlier careers, freelance and voluntary
work, the thing they built that nobody asked for, the languages, the failure they learned
from. People leave out the most relevant thing they have done because it does not match
their current title. Ask directly, more than once.

**4. Energy and interest.** What they read when nobody is paying, what they would do unpaid,
which part of the last job they would keep and which they would not repeat. Ask what drains
them; that decides more than what they are good at, and almost nobody volunteers it. Ask
what they have ruled out and what they believe they cannot do: the record tests those
claims. Ask whether their current employer has the work they are reaching for, and whether
they have asked.

**5. Constraints, as numbers.** Income floor as a figure rather than a feeling, gross or
net, per month or year. Runway in months, notice period, the place (cities and countries in
and out, remote or hybrid limits, relocation), travel they will accept. Work rights,
languages at working level, family and health. On-call, quota-carrying work, employment
against contracting, and the hours a week available for the search and for any build.

**6. The clock.** Their age, what they intend to be doing at the end of the next decade, and
what they are not willing to still be doing. Direction that ignores this ages badly in
exactly the way they are afraid of.

Treat what they say they want as a hypothesis, and test it against passes 1 to 4. Sometimes
the record says something the person does not.

A contradiction is two things that cannot both be true, such as their account against the
record or against another person's. A single instance in the record contradicts "I cannot"
or "I avoid". A want the record does not support is not one. Put each back to them once:
those found in passes 1 to 4 after pass 4, and those from passes 5 and 6 after pass 6.
